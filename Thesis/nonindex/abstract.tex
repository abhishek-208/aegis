\chapter*{Abstract}
Federated Learning (FL) allows distributed clients to collaboratively train a shared model without exposing their private data. However, this decentralized architecture leaves FL highly vulnerable to Byzantine attacks, where malicious actors submit poisoned updates to destroy the model's accuracy or plant hidden backdoors. While existing robust aggregation methods can filter out basic attacks, they frequently collapse when honest clients possess highly heterogeneous, Non-IID data distributions, as they mistakenly discard valuable specialist updates as malicious outliers.

In this thesis, we propose \textbf{\aegis{}}, a multi-layered Byzantine-resilient aggregation protocol explicitly designed to withstand sophisticated model-poisoning attacks in severe Non-IID environments. To break the deadlock between data heterogeneity and adversarial evasion, \aegis{} introduces a two-pass directional screening process. This architecture actively decontaminates the aggregator's reference point before applying an adaptive, variance-aware Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) filter. We further fortify the protocol by integrating a multi-dimensional credit scoring system that evaluates clients across three distinct axes: volume clipping to neutralize weight inflation, cosine-based credibility to measure directional trust, and cross-round reputation penalties to track historical reliability.

We comprehensively evaluate \aegis{} on the CIFAR-10 dataset under a severe 4-shard Non-IID partition at a $30\%$ Byzantine fraction, against a suite of adversaries spanning structural attacks (Label-Flipping, Sign-Flipping), volume manipulation (Volume Spam), and stealthy optimisation attacks (Inner Product Manipulation (IPM), ``A Little Is Enough'' (ALIE), and Sybil flooding). Our results show that \aegis{} attains a near-zero ``Byzantine Tax'' on honest Non-IID clients and is the strongest aggregator on the structural and volume-based attacks---achieving the highest accuracy under both Label-Flip and Volume Spam---while remaining competitive under Sign-Flip, IPM, and Sybil. We are equally explicit about its boundaries: \aegis{} survives IPM through credit-score dilution rather than detection, and collapses under the omniscient ALIE attack, a failure we show to be architectural rather than a matter of tuning. Across the Non-IID regime \aegis{} consistently outperforms the median-based baselines---the Coordinate-wise Median, Multi-Krum, and Bulyan---all at a linear $\mathcal{O}(Kd)$ aggregation cost.
